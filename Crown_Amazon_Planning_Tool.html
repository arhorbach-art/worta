The interview point this tab makes: margin percentage and relevance are two different questions. A spare part at 26% margin looks like the best product in the portfolio — until you see it contributes a few percent of channel profit, while a 13%-margin pallet truck carries a fifth of it. Percentages tell you how healthy a SKU is; absolute euros tell you what the channel actually lives on. Manage both, and never cut a "low-margin" SKU without checking which column it sits at the top of.

How to read it: low CTR with healthy conversion is a listing-image and price-point problem — people aren't clicking. Chart library unavailable offline — the confidence figure gives the same answer.
Why this matters more on B2B than consumer: your conversion counts are small — a pallet truck listing might see 30 orders a month, not 3,000. At those volumes an 18% "lift" is very often noise, and rolling it out teaches you nothing while quietly costing sales. This runs a two-proportion z-test: it asks how likely a gap this large is if the two versions were actually identical. Note the honest limitation — Amazon has no native A/B split for most sellers outside Manage Your Experiments, so before/after period tests are confounded by seasonality and rank drift. Treat a significant result as evidence, not proof.
